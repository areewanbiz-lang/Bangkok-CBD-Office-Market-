SECTION 01 • MACROECONOMIC HEADWINDS & DEMAND PILLARS

Macroeconomic Drivers & Concentrated Occupier Demand

Following GDP growth of 2.5% in 2024 and 2.4% in 2025, Thailand's 2026 economic growth was revised by the Bank of Thailand (BOT) to 2.3%, while KKP Research downgraded estimates to 1.3% amid 3.0% inflation. Four key headwinds have constrained tenant expansion: energy price volatility, softer export values ahead of U.S. reciprocal tariffs, a 8.1% YoY decline in Q2 2026 tourism arrivals (6.6 million), and public debt approaching the 70% GDP ceiling. Consequently, BOT's Business Sentiment Index (BSI) registered 46.1 in Q2 2026, keeping CFOs cautious.

1. MNC Flight-to-Quality & ESG

Multinationals bound by net-zero targets require LEED/WELL space. JLL reports 70%–75% of MNCs need floor plates >1,000 sq m. Key moves: HSBC Thailand & Baker McKenzie to One Bangkok T4 (Fl 30-33), GSK to Central Park Offices.

2. Chinese EV & Digital Inflows

BOI incentives and trade friction drove FDI into Thailand. ZDATA ($1.22B) & KDDI ($230M) digital investments, plus Sunwoda ($1.54B battery plant) and BYD, generate sales/HQ demand. BEV registrations surged 80% in 2025 (120,301 units, 19.4% market share).

3. SME Cost Rationalization

Domestic SMEs are executing overhead rationalization, migrating out of prime central corridors toward transit-linked non-CBD sub-markets or Grade B/C spaces (averaging THB 542/sq m) to minimize fixed real estate expenditure.

SECTION 02 • MACRO SUPPLY & ESG ABSORPTION

Structural Bifurcation (Two-Tier): The ESG Flight-to-Quality & Zero-Sum Cannibalization

Bangkok's commercial office market is experiencing a structural realignment rather than net expansion. High gross leasing volumes (134,000 sq m in Q2 2026) represent zero-sum relocation from aging, non-certified stock into new green-certified Grade A/A+ developments. Certified green stock expanded to 2.95 million sq m (43.7% of total market supply) by Q2 2026, capturing 100% of net market absorption.

Structural Divergence

ESG Mandates & Zero-Sum Cannibalization

Market absorption in Bangkok is heavily bifurcated. Multinational corporations bound by corporate net-zero pledges are migrating en masse into LEED- and WELL-certified prime buildings. This movement is not an organic expansion of total headcount, but a zero-sum cannibalization where green-certified stock gains space while aging non-green buildings suffer net tenant contraction.

SECTION 03 • BUILDING GRADE STRATIFICATION

"The Squeezed Middle" Paradox Across Office Building Tiers

Granular market data exposes a counter-intuitive structural stratification: Grade C offices maintain the highest occupancy rate in Bangkok (79.0%) due to zero new supply additions and cost-conscious domestic SMEs. Conversely, Grade B assets suffer the lowest occupancy (74.0%), squeezed between discounted Grade A net effective rates and budget-focused Grade C alternatives.

Grade B Tier (The Squeezed Middle)

THB 864 / sq m

Occupancy: 74.0% (-2.0 pp QoQ; lowest market-wide). Legacy Grade B buildings lack ESG specs and face severe pressure as tenants upgrade to Grade A via 20%–30% net effective discounts or downsize to Grade C.

Grade C Tier (Defensive Value)

THB 542 / sq m

Occupancy: 79.0% (-2.0 pp QoQ; highest market-wide). Insulated by a complete absence of new supply additions and sustained demand from cost-driven local SMEs and overhead-conscious businesses.

SECTION 07 • FINANCIAL MECHANICS & CONCESSION MODELING

The Net Effective Rent Gap

Thai office tenancies are commonly negotiated for 3-year terms, often with renewal provisions. Leases of immovable property over 3 years generally need registration to be enforceable beyond 3 years, and a standard registered lease may attract an aggregate ~1.1% registration fee and stamp duty — not an automatic surcharge, so counsel should confirm. Rent and separately contracted services may be taxed differently; a 40:60 split is not a statutory rule. A 2-month rent-free period on a 36-month lease is a 5.56% undiscounted reduction (6 months: 16.67%); with landlord-funded fit-out and turnkey works, published observations put true net effective rents 20%–30% below legacy contracts — indicative, not universal achieved rates.

SECTION 08 • MARKET OUTLOOK (2026–2030)

Pipeline Contraction & Market Stabilization

The heavy delivery cycle of 2024–2026 is drawing to a close. Total forward pipeline supply peaked at 1.10 million sq m in early 2025 and has contracted to 612,000 sq m through 2031 (56% under construction; only 197,000 sq m due in H2 2026). Beyond 2026, CBRE reports just 300,000 sq m of rental office space under construction through 2030, with One Bangkok Phase 2 and The Central Phaholyothin postponed, setting the stage for gradual vacancy absorption between 2027 and 2029.

Strategic Action Framework by Stakeholder Segment

For Corporate Occupiers
  • Peak Leverage Window: Q4 2026 – Q4 2027 represents the cyclical peak of tenant bargaining power.
  • Negotiate Concessions: Require landlord-funded turnkey fit-outs (saving THB 15,000–20,000/sq m upfront) and 6+ months rent-free.
  • ESG Flight: Leverage high vacancy in newly completed towers to secure green-certified space at net effective discounts.
For Asset Owners (10+ Yrs)
  • Green Retrofitting: Upgrade chilled-water VAV systems and secure LEED/WELL certifications to qualify for corporate RFPs.
  • Turnkey Suites: Subdivide floor plates into 150–500 sq m fitted suites to capture capex-averse SMEs.
  • Adaptive Reuse: Consider converting secondary assets without transit links into hospitality or medical facilities.
For Capital Investors
  • K-Shaped Recovery: Yield resilience will accrue strictly to transit-linked, green-certified mixed-use assets.
  • REIT Stability: Top-tier transit-linked REITs (e.g., GVREIT at ~7.6% trailing yield) offer defensive income.
  • Pipeline Relief: Acute supply pressure eases significantly after 2026, positioning core CBD assets for absorption.

PESTEL Analysis: Bangkok Office Market & GMM Grammy Place

Political Factors
  • BOI incentives, including over THB 137bn committed under EV3/EV3.5, draw Chinese and Japanese inflows into digital infrastructure and EV manufacturing.
  • U.S. reciprocal tariffs accelerate China-plus-one relocations, though exports were volatile after front-loading.
  • Public debt near the 70%-of-GDP ceiling limits fiscal stimulus and public-sector space expansion.
Economic Factors
  • GDP 2.5% (2024), 2.4% (2025); 2026 forecasts range from 1.3% (KKP) to 2.3% (BOT). BSI below 50: 47.7 (March 2026), 46.1 (Q2 2026).
  • CBD asking rent THB 977 (Grade A 1,268; Grade B 864, Q2 2026), but 6+ months rent-free and fit-out subsidies cut net effective rents 20%–30%.
  • Bare-shell fit-out of THB 15,000–20,000/sq m pushes SMEs to fitted space and Grade B/C; Sukhumvit rents fell to THB 898 to hold 80.0% occupancy.
Social & Workforce
  • Hybrid work drives consolidation alongside demand for hospitality-grade amenities (cafés, fitness, auditoriums, ergonomic coworking).
  • Location choice follows BTS/MRT interchanges and retail/lifestyle ecosystems.
  • Asoke–Phrom Phong–Thonglor stays the main expatriate corridor (1-bed condos THB 32,000–55,000/mo).
Technological Factors
  • Grade A/A+ towers run chilled-water VAV, smart turnstiles and destination-control lifts; 1990s Grade B stock (GMM Grammy Place, K Tower) runs water-cooled package A/C.
  • M&E upgrades are a prerequisite for 15+-year towers seeking certification or corporate RFPs.
  • Cloud and data-centre investment (ZDATA USD 1.22B, KDDI USD 230M) widens the tech tenant base.
Environmental & ESG
  • ESG mandates send all positive net absorption to green stock: +92,000 sq m vs −16,000 sq m non-green (Q2 2026).
  • Green stock reached 2.95M sq m (43.7%) by Q2 2026, up from 2.10M sq m (33.3%) in Q1 2025.
  • Uncertified Grade B buildings face MNC defections unless retrofitted.
Legal & Regulatory
  • Leases of immovable property over 3 years generally need registration to be enforceable beyond 3 years; an aggregate ~1.1% fee and stamp duty may apply to a standard registered lease, but not universally.
  • Base rent / service-charge splits must follow the executed lease; 7% VAT may apply to qualifying service charges; owners are assessed under the Land and Building Tax Act B.E. 2562 (2019).
  • A consolidated management mandate can simplify decisions, but at GMM Grammy Place the owner/title, lessor (Geurt Fah disclosed), management rights and capital-approval perimeter must be verified from records; marketing alone does not establish single-owner freehold.
